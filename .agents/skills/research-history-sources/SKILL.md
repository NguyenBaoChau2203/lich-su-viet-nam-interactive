# Research Vietnamese History Sources

## Workflow

1. Define the exact claim and the grade-12 learning purpose before searching.
2. Prefer primary and authoritative sources: Vietnamese government archives, museums, libraries, textbooks, universities, and peer-reviewed scholarship.
3. Verify unstable or disputed claims with at least two independent sources.
4. Record for every source: title, author or institution, URL or catalog reference, publication date when known, access date, and which claim it supports.
5. Classify each claim as `verified`, `approximate`, `disputed`, or `editorial`.
6. Keep quoted text short and exact. Paraphrase explanations and attach sources near claims.
7. Stop and mark a claim unresolved when evidence is insufficient; never fill gaps from memory.

## Map data

- Verify that a coordinate points to the intended historical location, not merely a modern administrative center.
- Store the coordinate source and note when historical boundaries or place names differ from today.
- Use an approximate area or explanatory note when point precision would mislead learners.

## Output

Return a compact claim table, source list, uncertainty notes, and fields ready for the data model. Separate sourced fact from editorial teaching copy.
